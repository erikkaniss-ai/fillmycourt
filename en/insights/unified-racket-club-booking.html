Booking strategy

The problem is not a lack of booking software. It is fragmentation between booking, CRM and distribution.

A club may use a marketplace, white-label app, its own website and different systems without wanting to rebuild everything at once. The value of a common layer is to turn those channels into one coherent operation.

One common inventory model

Venue, court, slot, price, availability and booking state need a consistent representation. Where a provider offers authorised access, an adapter can synchronise data; where it does not, the flow respects the official channel or uses native booking.

Booking should feed the Revenue Engine

Each booking can update preferred time, frequency, partners, lifecycle, match formation and attribution. The calendar stops being just a schedule and starts producing operating intelligence.

Distribute without getting locked in

External channels can continue bringing new demand. The goal is to measure acquisition/distribution cost and increase repeat direct booking where permitted and economically better.

The technical risk

Multi-source booking requires locking, idempotency, conflict prevention, cancellation rules and payment logic. A serious architecture does not promise two-way sync where the provider does not provide access.
